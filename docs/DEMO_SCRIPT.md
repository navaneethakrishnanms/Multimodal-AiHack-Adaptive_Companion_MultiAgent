# Demo video script

_Generated by `scripts/make_demo.py` (mode: **online**). Narration is built from the live system state and `eval_report.json`; re-record after changing either._

Target length 6–7 minutes (hard limit for the hackathon: 3–10). Voice: edge-tts `en-US-AriaNeural`. Browser recording: Playwright, 1920×1080, captions burned in.

## Scene 1 — Problem and idea  
*On screen:* title card  
*Narration (~68 words, ~28 s), one block per synchronised beat (the browser step runs when its block starts):*

> **intro** — Studying from slides, textbooks and lecture videos means jumping between files, and a general chatbot answers confidently without saying where the answer came from, or whether your own course even covers it. Study Companion builds one knowledge base from all your material. It answers only from it, with citations that open the exact page, slide or timestamp. And when you get a question wrong, it works out why.
>

## Scene 2 — Upload and ingestion  
*On screen:* Library: drop 5 files, build the topic map  
*Narration (~102 words, ~42 s), one block per synchronised beat (the browser step runs when its block starts):*

> **lib_upload** — Let's start with the material. I drop in 5 files from a sample machine learning course: notes, a slide deck, a lecture transcript, a textbook chapter as a P D F, and a recorded lecture video.
>
> **lib_wait** — There is no manual preprocessing. The P D F and the slides are read page by page and the images inside them are extracted as figures. The video is transcribed with Whisper into timestamped passages. A vision model describes each figure, so you can ask about it.
>
> **lib_map** — One click builds the topic map: 15 topics joined by 16 prerequisite links, with every passage tagged to them.
>

## Scene 4 — Declining what the material does not cover  
*On screen:* Tutor: off-material question, amber banner  
*Narration (~79 words, ~33 s), one block per synchronised beat (the browser step runs when its block starts):*

> **r_ask** — A tutor you can trust has to say when it does not know. The capital of France is not in this course, so the tutor declines and flags it in amber instead of improvising.
>
> **r_hold** — A general answer can still be requested, but it is shown separately and marked as outside your materials. On our held out test questions the refusal gate declines 67 percent of the off material queries, while still answering 95 percent of the in material ones.
>

## Scene 5 — Assessment with verification  
*On screen:* Practice: demo student, Regularization quiz  
*Narration (~64 words, ~27 s), one block per synchronised beat (the browser step runs when its block starts):*

> **q_gen** — Practice. This demo student is strong on the basics, but has a hidden gap. I pick the topic Regularization and generate a quiz.
>
> **q_hold** — Every question is tagged with its topic, difficulty and source, and carries a verified badge. The language model writes the questions, and an independent pass re-solves each one before you see it. A question whose key does not match is dropped.

## Scene 6 — Root cause diagnosis  
*On screen:* Wrong answer, root cause graph, probe, repair path  
*Narration (~127 words, ~53 s), one block per synchronised beat (the browser step runs when its block starts):*

> **rc_wrong** — I answer wrongly. The feedback shows the model answer, the explanation, the exact source to check, and the mastery estimate dropping.
>
> **rc_find** — Now the key idea. A wrong answer on Regularization may really be a gap in something it depends on. Root cause scores each prerequisite: weak or never tested prerequisites get the most blame.
>
> **rc_hold** — Overfitting has never been tested, so it gets 97 percent of the blame, while Mean Squared Error, which the student knows well, gets 3 percent. The graph lights up the path.
>
> **rc_probe** — Only two verified probe questions on Overfitting can change its mastery. The student misses both, and the diagnosis is confirmed.
>
> **rc_repair** — The repair path lists exactly what to read, with links to the notes and the lecture timestamp, followed by a re test.
>

## Scene 9 — Evaluation  
*On screen:* Evaluation dashboard from eval_report.json  
*Narration (~125 words, ~52 s), one block per synchronised beat (the browser step runs when its block starts):*

> **e_tab** — Finally, evaluation. Every number here comes from a script. Retrieval puts a relevant passage in the top four for 96 percent of 44 questions, with context precision 0.875 and recall 0.886.
>
> **e_gen** — For generation, faithfulness and answer relevancy are not measured in this run, because they need a working language model, and we do not estimate them.
>
> **e_chart** — For personalization we simulated thirty students per course whose hidden gaps follow the prerequisite graph. Root cause improves the F one score of diagnosed root causes over weakest first tracking by 0.332, with a ninety five percent interval from 0.172 to 0.494.
>
> **e_gain** — It does not win on every measure: its gain in true gap closure is not statistically significant at this sample size. We report that as it is.

## Scene 10 — Architecture  
*On screen:* architecture slide  
*Narration (~96 words, ~40 s), one block per synchronised beat (the browser step runs when its block starts):*

> **arch** — Under the hood, ingestion turns P D Fs, slides, audio and video into source linked units, with figures described by a vision model. A topic map with prerequisites tags every unit. Retrieval is T F I D F with a calibrated refusal gate, and the tutor sees only retrieved excerpts and must cite them. Quizzes are generated, independently verified, and de duplicated per student. The learner model is Bayesian knowledge tracing per topic, and Root cause adds the prerequisite graph on top of it. Everything has an offline fallback, so the app degrades instead of breaking.
>
